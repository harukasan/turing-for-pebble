# Shared C core

## Modes and memory contract

| Mode          | Grid      | Storage per cell                                     |   Fields | Row buffers |     Mask | Total caller allocation |
| ------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------: | ----------: | -------: | ----------------------: |
| 0             | 200 × 228 | one 16-bit word: A 7-bit linear, B 9-bit square-root | 91,200 B |     8,000 B | 45,600 B |               147,747 B |
| 1             | 100 × 114 | one unsigned 16-bit Q15 word per species             | 45,600 B |     4,000 B | 11,400 B |                63,231 B |
| 2, diagnostic | 100 × 114 | one 16-bit word packed as in mode 0                  | 22,800 B |     4,000 B | 11,400 B |                40,947 B |
| 3, watch      | 120 × 136 | one unsigned 16-bit Q15 word per species             | 65,280 B |     4,800 B | 16,320 B |                88,671 B |

The table holds for both models. FitzHugh–Nagumo runs only in the Q15 modes 1 and 3 and stores its two species in the same Q15 words. Mode 3 is the watch build. Its grid is not an integer fraction of the display, so it is shown with interpolated rendering (below). Totals include a 112-byte control structure (60 bytes in version 4, before the model number and the 10-entry parameter vector replaced the five Gray–Scott coefficients and the stops of the custom palette were added), a rendering area, and up to 3 bytes of alignment padding. The rendering area holds an 800-byte RGBA output row, a 516-byte display lookup table, a second 516-byte table of Q15 values in the packed modes, 600 bytes of column tables for interpolation (the left column, the weight, and the right column of every display column), and a scratch row of 2 bytes per grid column. Row buffers hold one 32-bit error diffusion residual row per species and, in the packed modes, four decoded Q24 rows per species (previous, current, next, and original first row, 32-bit each), the previous row being overwritten with the current row's Laplacian before it is decoded anew. The Q15 modes use the same area for a row of 20-fold Laplacian sum pairs, a row of new value pairs, and the old rows above and at the row and the original first row as 16-bit codes, and read the rows below in place. A watch build with `RD_AVOID` 0 has no mask area. Consumers must query `rd_bytes` and `rd_memory` instead of relying on these recorded values. The core performs no allocation. The caller owns a single contiguous block and frees the original pointer, not the potentially aligned state pointer returned by `rd_init`.

In the packed modes, rotating the previous, current, and next row pointers preserves the old field while writing in place, and each stored row is decoded once when it becomes the next row. In the Q15 modes only the row being rewritten is copied. In both, the saved first row supplies the periodic boundary when the last row is updated.

## Numerical definition, version 5

Version 5 adds a second model, FitzHugh–Nagumo, to the Q15 modes. The Gray–Scott definition is that of version 4, unchanged: every Gray–Scott field hash of version 4 is still in `tests/golden-hashes.txt`. Each field has one model, chosen at initialization (`rd_init_model`), with a parameter vector of Q15 integers (`RD_PARAM_MAX` = 10 entries, of which Gray–Scott uses 5 and FitzHugh–Nagumo 9).

### Gray–Scott

The Gray–Scott model uses an explicit Euler step and periodic boundaries:

```text
A' = clamp(A + dt × (Da × lap(A) − A × B² + feed × (1 − A)))
B' = clamp(B + dt × (Db × lap(B) + A × B² − (feed + kill) × B))
```

Its parameter vector is feed, kill, da, db, dt, each Q15 in [0, 32768], where `RD_Q15_ONE` = 32,768 means 1. Storage rounding uses the error diffusion described below. The arithmetic of a step depends on the storage.

The packed modes 0 and 2 decode their codes to Q24 concentrations, where `RD_VALUE_ONE` = 16,777,216 means 1, and compute as in versions 2 and 3, rounding to nearest with halfway values away from zero. The nine-point Laplacian (`laplacian_row` in `core/rd.c`) is evaluated on Q24 values as `(4 × axial_sum + diagonal_sum − 20 × center) / 20`, rounded. These exact rational weights preserve uniform fields exactly. The reaction is `A × B` rounded to Q24, then times `B` rounded to Q24. Each rate is accumulated as a Q39 sum of exact products of a Q15 coefficient and a Q24 concentration, with the reaction scaled by 2¹⁵. The rate times `dt` is a Q54 product rounded once to Q24 and added to the concentration.

The Q15 modes 1 and 3 compute on their Q15 codes with 32-bit arithmetic (`react_codes`). The Laplacian stays the exact 20-fold integer sum `S = 4 × axial_sum + diagonal_sum − 20 × center` of the codes (`laplacian_sums`). Each diffusion coefficient is folded with the 1/20 of the Laplacian into `fold(D) = (D + 10) / 20` in integer division, so the core computes with the effective coefficient `fold(D) × 20 / 32768`, within 10/32768 of the requested one (0.99976 of the request for Da = 1 and Db = 0.5). The reaction `A × B²` is exact to the Q30 unit: `A × B` splits at bit 15 into `hi × 2¹⁵ + lo`, and the reaction is `hi × B + floor(lo × B / 2¹⁵)`. The rates, in Q30, are

```text
rate_A = fold(Da) × S_A − A B² + feed × (1 − A)
rate_B = fold(Db) × S_B + A B² − (feed + kill) × B
```

`rate_A` always fits a signed 32-bit integer, because `fold(Da) × S_A` stays below 2³⁰ in magnitude. Of `rate_B` only the final subtraction can leave the range, when `feed + kill` exceeds 1, and the result then saturates at −2³¹, which the clamp below turns into B = 0 in every such case, as the exact value would. With `dt` = 1 the new value in Q24 is the code times 2⁹ plus the rate rounded half up to Q24, `(rate >> 6) + ((rate >> 5) & 1)`. With another `dt` it is the code times 2⁹ plus `(rate × dt + 2²⁰) >> 21` in 64 bits, the same rounding. The result then passes through error diffusion, which clamps it to the codable range of its storage.

### FitzHugh–Nagumo

FitzHugh–Nagumo runs in the Q15 modes 1 and 3 with the same explicit Euler step, periodic boundaries, and nine-point Laplacian:

```text
u' = u + dt × (Du × lap(u) + ru × (u − u³ − v + k) − pull × (u − rest))
v' = v + dt × (Dv × lap(v) + rv × (u − av × v))
```

u is the displayed and masked species, v the inhibitor. `pull` is 0 away from a mask (below). The parameter vector is du, dv, ru, rv, av, k, dt, rest, init (`RD_FHN_PARAMS` = 9). k and rest are Q15 in [−32768, 32768], ru is Q15 in [0, 8192] (`RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, 0.25, which the int32 bound below needs), init is the integer 0 or 1, and the others are Q15 in [0, 32768]. rest is the resting value of u: the initial uniform field, the value masked cells hold, and the target of the pull. init selects the initial condition.

**Storage.** Both species x ∈ [−2, 2] are stored as the Q15 code of the fraction s = (x + 2) / 4, so x = 4 × code / 32768 − 2 and the code minus 16,384 is x in Q13 (`x13`). Plane 1 holds u and plane 0 holds v, so `rd_get` species 1 is u and species 0 is v, each returned as the fraction s in Q24. The codes clamp x to [−2, 2]. Error diffusion and its nearest-code rounding are those of the Gray–Scott Q15 modes.

**Arithmetic** (`react_fhn_codes` in `core/rd.c`), in 32 bits on the codes, with `>>` an arithmetic shift (a floor):

```text
u13 = code_u − 16384            v13 = code_v − 16384
uu  = (u13 × u13) >> 13         uuu = (uu × u13) >> 13
k13 = (k + 2) >> 2              rest13 = (rest + 2) >> 2
rate_u = fold(Du) × S_u + ru × (u13 − uuu − v13 + k13) − pull × (u13 − rest13)
rate_v = fold(Dv) × S_v + rv × (u13 − ((av × v13) >> 15))
```

`S` and `fold(D) = (D + 10) / 20` are the 20-fold Laplacian sum of the codes and the folded diffusion coefficient of the Gray–Scott Q15 modes. k and rest are rounded to Q13 every step, so the effective values are within 2/32768 of the request, in steps of 4/32768. A Q15 coefficient times a Q13 value of x is directly the Q30 rate of the stored fraction: s changes by a quarter of x, and Q15 to Q13 is the same factor of 4. The new value is formed as in the Gray–Scott Q15 modes: the code times 2⁹ plus the rate rounded half up to Q24 with dt = 1, or `(rate × dt + 2²⁰) >> 21` in 64 bits otherwise.

**Both rates fit a signed 32-bit integer without saturation.** `|fold(D) × S| ≤ 1638 × 655,360 = 1,073,479,680 < 2³⁰`. For |x| ≤ 2, |x − x³| ≤ 6, so `|u13 − uuu| ≤ 49,152`: the maximum is reached at |x| = 2, where the floors are exact, and one Q13 step inward lowers |x − x³| by about 11 units while the floors move it by at most 3. The bracket of `ru` is then at most 49,152 + 16,384 + 8,192 = 73,728 in magnitude, which `ru ≤ 8192` turns into at most 603,979,776. With a pull of at most 8,192 (the mask uses at most 4,096), `|pull × (u13 − rest13)| ≤ 8,192 × 24,576 < 2²⁸`. The sum is at most 1,878,786,048 < 2³¹. For v, `|u13 − ((av × v13) >> 15)| ≤ 32,768`, so `|rate_v| ≤ 1,073,479,680 + 32,768 × 32,768 = 2,147,221,504 < 2³¹`. The oracle in `tests/core.c` computes both rates in 64 bits from this definition and asserts the bound, including the extreme cells of either sign.

**Initialization.** `rd_init_model` fills u = rest (code `16384 + rest13`) and v = rest / av as `v13 = rest13 × 32768 / av` in C integer division (truncation toward zero), 0 if av is 0, clamped to [−16384, 16384]. With init 0 the 24 LCG disks of Gray–Scott are then seeded, each writing u = rest + 0.5 (the resting code plus 4,096) and leaving v, which is also what `rd_seed` writes. With init 1 no LCG is used: a broken wave is written in display coordinates, u = 1 (code 24,576) in display rows 190–197 and v = 1 in rows 174–189, both in columns 0–99, each grid cell sampling the display coordinate it covers. The refractory v band lets the wave run only downward, and its free ends curl into a pair of counter-rotating spirals below the date line. A torus cannot hold a single spiral, so a pair is the smallest defect set. Q15 codes carry no dither, so a uniform field stays exactly uniform, and one whose rest is not an exact fixed point relaxes uniformly.

**Masked cells** hold u at the resting code `16384 + rest13`, which `rd_mask` writes at once and every step writes without encoding, with the share rules of Gray–Scott B. v is computed and encoded as usual. At mask levels 1 to 5, u is pulled toward rest with `pull = RD_MASK_PULL × (5 − level) / 5` in C integer division, `RD_MASK_PULL` = 4,096 (0.125), so level 5 has no pull and rows away from the mask skip it. Holding u at the dark plateau −1 instead would pull v of the neighboring cells below its rest and let a bright band rise along the digits, and in the excitable regime any value other than rest would turn the digits into a wave source. Fixing u at rest avoids both by construction. These alternatives were reasoned out, not run. The strength of the pull was measured ([Parameter search](#parameter-search)).

**Rendering** maps the Q24 value of the displayed species through `intensity = clamp(round_shift((value − offset) × gain, 9), 0, 32768)`, with offset 0 and gain 3 for Gray–Scott (3 B, bit for bit the rendering of version 4) and offset 2²³ and gain 4 for FitzHugh–Nagumo, so the intensity is u: u ∈ [0, 1] spans the palette, and u ≤ 0, the resting value of the excitable regime and the dark phase of the Turing patterns, shows the background color.

### History

Version 1 rounded every product to Q15 and stored 8-bit codes with stochastic rounding. That definition is retained only as a baseline inside `tests/precision.c`. Version 2 introduced the codes, the error diffusion, and the Q24 arithmetic with a dithered threshold in every mode. Version 3 differed from it only in the Q15 modes, whose codes round to the nearest code without the dither. Version 4 moved the Q15 modes to the 32-bit arithmetic above, which the harness measured at the same Float32 error as version 3 and the watch at 21% less time per step with the row loop of that time (27% with the later three-pass loop), and left the packed modes as in version 3. [Storage precision study](precision-optimization.md) records the comparisons. Version 5 added FitzHugh–Nagumo and the parameter vector, and left every Gray–Scott result of version 4 unchanged.

### Storage codes

Modes 0 and 2 store one 16-bit word per cell, `(codeA << 9) | codeB`. Modes 1 and 3 store one Q15 word per species.

| Storage      | Decode to Q24                         | Floor code of a Q24 value v                                      | Largest value       |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| A, 7 bits    | `round(code × 2²⁴ / 127)`             | `v × 127 / 2²⁴` truncated, plus one if the next code decodes ≤ v | 1                   |
| B, 9 bits    | `code² × 64`, that is `(code / 512)²` | `isqrt(v / 64)`                                                  | 511² / 512² ≈ 0.996 |
| Q15, 16 bits | `code × 512`                          | `v / 512`                                                        | 1                   |

The floor code is the largest code that decodes to at most v. Error diffusion decides between it and the next code. The square-root companding of B spaces codes in proportion to √B: near B = 0.05 the step is 0.0009, near 0.25 it is 0.0020, and near 1 it is 0.0039, compared with 0.0020 everywhere for a 9-bit linear code. The 18-bit domain of `isqrt` covers every codable B value.

### Error diffusion

Every write uses Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion (`encode_cell` and `finish_row` in `core/rd.c`). The value to be written, plus the shares it received from already written neighbors, is clamped to the codable range. For the packed codes of modes 0 and 2, its fraction of the step from the floor code to the next code is compared with a dithered threshold `(2¹⁴ + random) / 2¹⁶`, where `random` is 15 bits of `mix32(cell_index ^ mix32(seed ^ mix32(step)))`, the low bits for A and the bits from 16 for B. The next code is taken when the fraction is at least the threshold, so the threshold is uniform over [1/4, 3/4) and exact codes never move. The Q15 codes of modes 1 and 3 take the next code when the fraction is at least 1/2, with no dither: at that resolution the study measured no front pinning and a smaller error without it. The residual, the clamped value minus the decoded code, is divided with C integer division: 7/16 to the right neighbor, 3/16 to the lower-left neighbor, 5/16 to the neighbor below, and the remainder to the lower-right neighbor. Cells are written row by row from the top and column by column from the left. The lower-left share of column 0 wraps to the last column and the lower-right share of the last column wraps to column 0 of the next row. The right share of the last column carries into column 0 of the next row. Shares held between cells are reset at the start of each step. The residual rows persist: shares given below the last row are applied to row 0 of the next step. A uniform field has zero residuals and remains exact.

### Masked cells

`rd_mask` takes a cell bitmap and derives a mask level for every cell: 0 in a masked cell, else the chessboard distance in cells to the nearest masked cell, capped at `RD_MASK_RAMP` (5). Distances do not wrap around the field edges. Only the band of rows within 4 rows of a masked cell is derived and read. The levels take one byte per cell (the mask column above). A ring of horizontal distance rows in the saved-row buffers, which are free between steps, derives them in `rd_mask`. Deriving them during every step instead saved memory but cost 13% of the step time in mode 1 on the watch, and a 3-bit store left too little heap at 134 × 152, so both were dropped. A watch build that defines `RD_AVOID` as 0 has no mask area and `rd_mask` returns −1.

In Gray–Scott the kill rate of a cell rises linearly from the parameter kill at level 5 to `RD_MASK_KILL` (2,458, 0.075) at level 0: `kill + (2458 − kill) × (5 − level) / 5` with C integer division. The reaction of every cell uses its own kill. A is computed and encoded as usual everywhere. B of a masked cell is held at code 0: `rd_mask` writes it at once, and every step writes 0 without encoding. The B shares it received from the row above and the right share of the cell to its left are dropped, it gives no share of its own, and its B residual entry is the lower-right share of the cell to its left plus the lower-left share of the cell to its right, which pass through to the row below.

With A left free and the kill raised around the mask, B cannot grow near the digits and the pattern fades out a few cells before them. Holding A at 1 as well was tried first: the mask then supplies fresh A to its edge and a bright band forms along it. Only the rows that contain a level below 5 read the levels, so a step without a mask is unchanged. `rd_seed` skips masked cells. The levels are not part of `rd_hash`, but the golden hashes with a clock mask pin this rule. FitzHugh–Nagumo uses the same levels and share rules with u held at its resting code and pulled toward rest instead of a raised kill rate ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)).

Error diffusion makes the write deterministic and order dependent. The full-screen oracle in `tests/core.c` reproduces the same order. It conserves the encoded mass of a row exactly, which `tests/core.c` checks, and moves the quantization error to high spatial frequencies that diffusion removes within a few steps. The dither keeps slowly moving fronts from being pinned by the coarse packed codes without giving up that conservation. Both are measured in [Storage precision study](precision-optimization.md).

## Initialization

The initial Gray–Scott equilibrium is A=1 and B=0. A 32-bit LCG places 24 disks in a common 200 × 228 display coordinate system. Disk radii range from 4 through 9 display pixels. Grid cells sample their corresponding display coordinates, with periodic distance used at edges. Seeded values are A=0.5 and B=0.25, written directly as codes: 64/127 ≈ 0.504 and exactly 0.25 in the packed modes, and exact Q15 values in modes 1 and 3. Grid resolution still changes the physical scale of emergent patterns. FitzHugh–Nagumo starts from u = rest and v = rest / av and seeds the same disks with u = rest + 0.5, or writes its broken wave with init 1 ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)).

A mask has one bit per grid cell in rows of `(width + 7) / 8` bytes, cell x in bit x % 8 of byte x / 8.

`core/clock_mask.c` builds the mask of the clock digits, shared by the watch and the Web. `cm_build(mask, width, height, font, hour, minute, year, month, day, halo, date)` draws HH:MM and, when `date` is nonzero, YYYY.MM.DD with the glyphs of font set `font` (`CM_FONT_LECO` or `CM_FONT_BITHAM`) as the watch places them, each line centered by the sum of its advances. The time line's text box starts at `cm_time_top(font, date)`: the layout's time top with the date, and without it the top that centers the ink of the time glyphs on the display, which `scripts/gen-clock-glyphs.py` computes into the layout table. It accepts grid widths from 50 to 200 with `height = width × 228 / 200`. Each glyph pixel is widened by a square of `halo` display pixels (at most `CM_MAX_HALO`), clipped to the display, and every grid cell holding the center of such a pixel, `floor((2x + 1)·w / 400)`, is marked. That equals the earlier `x·w / 200` rule at widths 100 and 200. It returns −1 without writing for arguments out of range. `cm_draw(row, context, color, font, hour, minute, year, month, day, date)` sets the same glyph pixels in a display through a row callback, which the watch uses to draw its clock text. The rows map by the same rule with the height and 456. `cm_draw` returns −1 for a NULL row callback. `cm_bytes`, `cm_layout` (font keys and text boxes), and `cm_font_available` complete it. The glyph tables in `core/clock_glyphs.h` are generated. A build that defines `RD_FONT` compiles only that set.

The analog face uses angles in units of 1/1440 of a turn (`CM_TURN`), clockwise from 12 o'clock. `cm_build_analog(mask, width, height, font, hour_angle, minute_angle, year, month, day, halo, date)` draws the hour hand (length 54, radius 3), the minute hand (length 80, radius 2), and the center disk (radius 5) around display pixel (100, 104), then, when `date` is nonzero, the date line of the font set centered at `cm_analog_date_top(font)`, and marks cells with the halo and cell rule of `cm_build`. A hand's tip is the center plus `L × sin(a)` across and minus `L × cos(a)` down, each rounded half away from zero from a Q14 sine. The sine table `CM_SIN_Q14` in `core/clock_trig.h` (generated by `scripts/gen-clock-trig.py`) holds `round(sin(2πk / 1440) × 16384)` for k = 0 to 360, 722 bytes, and the other quadrants fold onto it, so mirrored angles give exactly mirrored tips. A hand is the capsule of the segment from the center to the tip: a pixel belongs to it when its squared distance to the segment is at most the squared radius, tested in exact 32-bit integer arithmetic without division. Each row of a capsule is one run, scanned from the pixel nearest the segment. A halo 0 mask of width 200 has one cell per display pixel, so it is exactly the pixel bitmap of the face, which the Web draws, and `cm_draw_analog` sets the same pixels, which `tests/core.c` checks with the geometry of all 720 times, mirror symmetry, and the capsule against the distance test. `cm_sweep_angle` moves `elapsed_ms / duration_ms` of the way with a quadratic ease-out, `1 − (1 − t)²` in steps of 1/1024, along the signed shorter difference, rounding the offset as `(delta × eased + 512) / 1024` with C division. It returns `from` until the sweep starts and exactly `to` from its end. A build without `RD_FACE` compiles both faces, as the watch builds do for the face setting. One that defines `RD_FACE` as 0 compiles no analog code, and one that defines it as 1 compiles only the date line of its font set, so `cm_build` and `cm_draw` return −1 there. The watch compiles the analog code for size. The golden hashes with an analog mask pin the table and the rasterizer.

Rendering flags are `RD_ROW_QUANTIZE` (1, round each channel to the RGB2 levels, implied by `rd_row_rgb2`) and `RD_ROW_BILINEAR` (2). Without `RD_ROW_BILINEAR`, each display pixel shows the grid cell that covers it, exactly as before the flag existed, which `tests/core.c` checks against recorded hashes. With it, the displayed species (B, or u) is interpolated at the pixel center before coloring. Display pixel x lies at grid position u = ((2x + 1)·w − 200) / 400. With q = floor(256u), the left cell is floor(q / 256) modulo w, periodic like the field, and the right cell gets the weight q mod 256. Rows use the same rule with h and 228. The two grid rows are first lerped vertically in Q15 with rounding half up, then each pixel lerps horizontally the same way. The result is colored through a 513-entry table of 64-value Q15 buckets, with the exact color computed for the few buckets that straddle a color step. Because interpolation happens before the RGB2 quantization, the color steps that outline the stripes fall at display-pixel positions between cells, so a grid that is not dot-by-dot shows smooth stripe edges instead of uneven 1- and 2-pixel blocks.

Palettes (`RD_PALETTE_*`) are 0 lime, 1 cyan, 2 monochrome, 3 viridis, 4 magma, 5 plasma, 6 inferno, 7 cividis, 8 turbo, and 9 custom, with `RD_PALETTE_COUNT` after them. The display intensity is B × 3 for Gray–Scott and u for FitzHugh–Nagumo (the Q24 value minus the model's offset, times its gain), rounded to Q15 and clamped to [0, 1.0] ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)). Monochrome is white from an intensity of 45% and black below it. Every other palette has 2 to 8 RGB stops (`RD_PALETTE_MAX_STOPS`) equally spaced from intensity 0 to 1.0. With n stops the position is intensity × (n − 1), its multiples of 2^15 select the segment (the last one at 1.0), and each channel is `low + floor(((high − low) × fraction + 2^14) / 2^15)`, the nearest integer to the exact interpolation with halves rounded up. Lime and cyan have two stops and keep the colors of the former endpoint formula, which `tests/core.c` checks against the hashes of the earlier renderer. The built-in stops are defined in `lib/palettes.ts`, and `scripts/gen-palettes.mjs` writes them to the generated `core/palettes.h`, whose staleness `mise run test-core` checks. The colormaps are eight samples of matplotlib's 256-entry tables, with their origins and licenses in [licenses/palettes.txt](../licenses/palettes.txt). `rd_palette(state, rgb, count)` sets the stops of the custom palette from `count` RGB triples and drops a lookup table built for it. `rd_init` gives the custom palette the lime stops. `rd_row_rgb2` fills a lookup table from the B code (or, in modes 1 and 3, from 64-code buckets with a fallback for the few buckets that straddle a color step) the first time a palette is requested and reuses it afterwards. The model of a state is fixed from `rd_init_model` on, so the tables never need to be rebuilt for another model. The watch renders its rows straight into the framebuffer with `rd_row_rgb2_into`. `core/rd.h` also defines `RD_VERSION`, `RD_Q15_ONE`, `RD_VALUE_BITS`, `RD_VALUE_ONE`, `RD_DISPLAY_WIDTH`, `RD_DISPLAY_HEIGHT`, `RD_ROW_BYTES`, `RD_SPECIES_A` / `RD_SPECIES_B`, the model numbers `RD_MODEL_GRAY_SCOTT`, `RD_MODEL_FHN`, and `RD_MODEL_COUNT`, the vector lengths `RD_PARAM_MAX`, `RD_GRAY_SCOTT_PARAMS`, and `RD_FHN_PARAMS`, `RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, and the mask constants `RD_MASK_RAMP`, `RD_MASK_KILL`, and `RD_MASK_PULL` for C callers. A returned rendering row is overwritten by the next row call. The Web adapter copies each row into a persistent ImageData. The watch adapter writes RGB2 values directly into the OS framebuffer and owns no full-screen image.

Validation occurs before mutations. Invalid coefficients (a vector of the wrong length or with an entry out of its range), unknown models, FitzHugh–Nagumo in a packed mode, seed coordinates, step counts, output parameters, unsupported modes, and undersized initialization blocks leave state unchanged. Callers must supply live owned memory and a valid state pointer. Arbitrary dangling pointers are outside the C API contract. The API is not thread-safe for simultaneous use of one state.

## Implementation notes

These choices change no result. `tests/golden-hashes.txt` records the field hashes of the definition and `scripts/check-optimization.sh` checks every build against them.

- Each row's Laplacians are computed in one pass, sharing the vertical sums `up[x] + down[x]` between neighboring columns, with the wrapped first and last columns handled outside the loop: in the packed modes over the decoded rows, writing over the row above, and in the Q15 modes as 20-fold sums of the codes written to alternate entries of a row of pairs (`laplacian_sums` with a stride of 2).
- The coefficients, the residual row pointers, and the diffusion shares live in a stack context during a step, so the stores into the planes cannot force the compiler to reload them.
- `decode_a` is a 128-entry table of the rounded division, and `floor_b` normalizes its argument with a leading-zero count, takes a lower bound from a 256-entry table, and increments at most twice.
- Products of the non-negative concentrations are rounded without a sign test, and the dithered comparison uses 32-bit products except for the packed A step, whose products need 64 bits.
- In the packed modes, with `dt` = 1 the rate is rounded by 15 bits directly, which equals rounding the `dt` product by 30 bits.
- In the packed modes, A B rounded to Q24 is at most 1.0, so the reaction's second product is a single 32 × 32 → 64 bit multiply.
- The nearest Q15 code, halfway upward, is computed directly as `(value + 256) >> 9` instead of from the floor code and a comparison.
- The Q15 modes step on the codes themselves (`step_codes`). The stored rows below the row being written are still old and are read in place, only the old row being rewritten is copied as 16-bit codes, and the 20-fold Laplacian sums are taken on the codes into a row of pairs. Each row is then written in three passes with few live values each, so that the Cortex-M keeps them in registers: `react_row` computes the new values of both species into a row of pairs, and `encode_row` writes one species with its error diffusion. On the watch the three passes took 13.1 ms per step against 14.25 ms for one loop over both species, whose spills cost about a fifth of its instructions. On a desktop CPU the single loop is faster, so the shared core pays about 1.5 times the host step time for the watch gain. Fusing the Laplacian sums into the cell loop, unrolling it twice, compiling for the Cortex-M33, and a branch-free form of the 64-bit rounding each measured slower or equal on the watch. The passes are inlined twice, for rows inside and outside the mask band, so rows away from the digits run without the level lookups. The packed modes keep one loop over both species (`update_row`).
- The interpolated renderer takes its column tables (left and right columns, weights) and the value table once per row, and lerps as `l + floor(((r − l)·w + 128) / 256)`, which equals the two-weight form with one multiply. `rd_row_rgb2_into` writes a row straight into a caller buffer such as a framebuffer row.
- The value that error diffusion gives the cell to the left below is held in a register and stored once, when the below-left share of the next cell is known, instead of a read-modify-write per cell. The oracle in `tests/core.c` keeps the plain form of the rule on its own residual row.
- With nearest sampling, widths 200 and 100 map cells to pixels by a shift. The interpolated renderer has a loop specialized for 120 cells, where display pixels 5k to 5k + 4 have the constant weights 204, 102, 0, 153, and 51, and takes the B value of a packed code as `code² >> 3`. `render_rgb2`, `value_argb8`, `fill_value_table`, and the glyph traversal are kept out of line so that the watch build holds one copy of each, while the row passes are always inlined. A watch build defines `RD_MODE`, which folds the storage test to a constant, and `RD_RENDER_FLAGS`, which fixes the rendering flags.
- Error diffusion computes the right, lower-left, and lower shares by integer division by 16 with truncation toward zero. This gives the same shares for every Q15 rounding error in [−256, 255] as the former 512-entry table, while removing its 2,048 bytes of static data.
- Optimizing GCC builds, the watch builds among them, compile the clock mask functions (`distance_row`, `advance_ring`, `ring_levels`, and `rd_mask`) with `optimize("Os")`, because mask updates run at launch and on clock changes, outside the step loop. On the watch this saved 828 B in modes 1 and 3, and one diagnostic run of each build measured 0.16 ms more per `rd_mask` call ([Validation](validation.md#mask-size-optimization)). A call from the step loop into these functions would also run at `-Os`. `-O0` builds leave them unoptimized, so `scripts/check-optimization.sh` compares an unoptimized reference with the size-optimized code, and the host tests run the mask at the watch's optimization level. Clang, and so Emscripten, ignores the GCC attribute with a warning that `-Werror` builds reject. Its `minsize` counterpart saved only 39 B of Wasm code in an estimate with clang 18 instead of `emcc`, so clang builds compile the mask functions at the command-line level.
- `react_row` chooses the model once per row and runs one cell loop per model, both inlined for rows inside and outside the mask band. Outside the band the FitzHugh–Nagumo pull is the constant 0, so its product disappears. `encode_row` stores the resting code of the model in masked cells. The per-step coefficients of both models live in the stack context, and `advance_code` forms the new Q24 value of either model from its rate.
- A build that defines `RD_MODEL` compiles only that model: the model tests fold to constants, so the compiler drops the other model's cell loops. `RD_MODEL` 1 (FitzHugh–Nagumo) with a packed `RD_MODE` is an `#error`, and a packed `RD_MODE` without `RD_MODEL` folds the model to Gray–Scott, since the packed modes run only Gray–Scott. Without `RD_MODEL`, as in the default watch build, the model is read from the state.
- `core/rd_bench.c` times the phases of a step (decode, Laplacian, reaction, and the whole step) and a full interpolated frame on the watch (`RD_BUILD_DEFINES=RD_BENCH`) and on the host (`tests/bench.c`). Only the watch numbers decide, because the host ranks the phases differently.

The existing Float32 implementation remains in `lib/simulation.ts` as a reference with its original tests. The Web can explicitly select it through `lib/float-simulation.ts` for same-resolution visual comparisons. Pebble builds use only the C implementation.

## Second model

### Choice of FitzHugh–Nagumo

The second model had to add looks that Gray–Scott does not give, fit the fixed-point core and its [0, 1] storage, and run in the watch's time budget. The candidates compared were:

| Model                          | Why it was not chosen                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gierer–Meinhardt               | A division in the reaction and an unbounded range of the activator                                           |
| Brusselator, Schnakenberg      | Values well above 1 and a Hopf region next to the Turing region                                               |
| Swift–Hohenberg, Cahn–Hilliard | Fourth-order derivatives, which need a very small dt for the explicit step                                    |
| McCabe multi-scale Turing      | Several blurs per step at different scales, too slow for the watch                                            |
| Barkley                        | A simplified FitzHugh–Nagumo that gives spirals only                                                          |
| Kondo–Miura linear saturation  | The lightest alternative, recorded as a fallback                                                              |

FitzHugh–Nagumo has two species, a polynomial reaction, and bounded values, which an affine map places in the existing [0, 1] storage. With one reaction term it gives both the Turing regime (stationary stripes and spots of even width) and the excitable regime (rotating spirals). The uniform resting field of the Q15 codes carries no noise, so the Turing patterns grow out of the 24 seed disks, as Gray–Scott does, rather than rising from the whole field at once.

### Parameter search

The presets were chosen on the Float32 prototype (`lib/fhn-simulation.ts`) with `scripts/fhn-explore.mjs`, which runs a parameter set on the 120 × 136 watch grid under the LECO clock mask at 13:57 2046-08-29 with a 1-pixel halo, prints fill, amplitude, feature size, motion, chessboard, halo, and connected-region measures at each checkpoint, and writes the renders. The targets were features of 6 to 12 cells, a fill of 0.9 by 1,500 steps, no chessboard, no bright band along the digits, and for the spiral a pattern that still turns after 10,000 steps without the digits becoming a wave source. The Q15 values are those of `lib/presets.ts`.

| Preset        | du   | dv  | ru     | rv      | av  | k     | rest    | init | Look                                                         |
| ------------- | ---- | --- | ------ | ------- | --- | ----- | ------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `fhn-stripes` | 0.05 | 1   | 0.01   | 0.025   | 0.6 | 0     | 0       | 0    | stationary labyrinth, u up to 0.74, lit runs of 7.4 cells    |
| `fhn-hex`     | 0.04 | 1   | 0.0175 | 0.04375 | 0.6 | −0.22 | −0.2925 | 0    | stationary bright spots, about 76 on the watch grid          |
| `fhn-spiral`  | 0.2  | 0   | 0.25   | 0.0125  | 1   | −0.3  | −0.6694 | 1    | a counter-rotating spiral pair below the date, keeps turning |

All three use dt = 1.

**Stripes.** The plan's starting point (Du 0.1, Dv 1, ru 0.05, rv 0.125, av 0.6) gave a clean labyrinth whose u reached only 0.52 to 0.55, too dim for the palette, which maps u from 0 to 1. With v following u through its fast diffusion, the stripe amplitude A satisfies about A² ≈ 4/3 × (1 − 2√(c Du) + c av Du) for Dv = 1 and c = rv / ru, and the wavelength grows as ru^(−1/2) at fixed Du and c. A sweep of Du {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} × Dv {0.5, 1} × ru {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} × rv/ru {2.5, 4} × av {0.4, 0.6, 0.8} reached u = 0.9 with Du = 0.02 and Dv = 1, but that far from the onset the seed disks froze into rings and dots. Sweeps of Du 0.01 to 0.1, ru 0.01 to 0.03, rv/ru 2 to 3, and av {0.6, 0.8} to 5,000 steps, counting the connected lit and dark regions, picked the brightest setting that still forms a labyrinth: u up to 0.74, 16 lit and 3 dark regions, a fill of 0.98 at 1,500 steps, and no motion by 5,000 steps. rv/ru = 2.5 keeps the uniform state clearly damped (ru − rv av = −0.005) where rv/ru = 2 was brighter but only just damped.

**Spots.** On the stripes setting, k from −0.3 to 0.3 with rest at the matching fixed point (u − u³ − u/av + k = 0) gave dark holes in a bright field for k > 0 and bright spots on a dark field for k < 0, mostly spots from |k| = 0.2, and ever slower growth with |k| (a fill of 0.88 at 1,500 steps for k = −0.2 and 0.39 for −0.3). Raising ru and rv in proportion speeds the growth up. Sweeps of Du 0.03 to 0.07, ru 0.01 to 0.02, rv/ru 2 to 2.5, av {0.6, 0.8}, and k {−0.15, −0.2, −0.22, −0.25} to 10,000 steps picked Du 0.04, ru 0.0175, k −0.22: a fill of 0.96 at 1,500 steps and about 76 spots, a few of them elongated, stationary by 10,000 steps. rest is the fixed point of these parameters, so the masked cells show the same background as the field and no halo forms.

**Spiral.** The plan's starting point (Du 1, Dv 0, ru 0.25, rv 0.015, av 1, k −0.5) lost its initial wave within 100 steps. A plane-front test showed fronts failing at k = −0.5 and moving at 0.24 cells per step at k = −0.3 and 0.3 at k = −0.2, whose fixed point −0.585 lies next to the knee at −1/√3. Sweeps of Du {0.5, 1} × rv {0.01, 0.02, 0.03} × k {−0.3, −0.35} lost the wave at rv 0.03, broke it up at 0.02, and at rv 0.01 turned but with waves about a screen apart. Du {0.15, 0.25, 0.35} × rv {0.0075, 0.01, 0.0125} × k {−0.25, −0.3} all kept turning for 10,000 steps. rv 0.0125 gave the narrowest bands among them (lit runs of 7 to 10 cells at k −0.3), and Du 0.2 lies between the tighter spirals of Du 0.15 and the wider ones of 0.25. The initial wave was first placed at display row 150, which put one core under the date, so it moved to row 190. ru stays at 0.25, within `RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, so the saturation the plan kept in reserve was not needed. rest is the stable root of u³ = k.

**Mask pull.** `RD_MASK_PULL` values of 0, 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 were compared by the fraction of cells within 1 or 2 cells of the digits with u > 0.5 at 5,000 steps. Stripes and spots needed only 1/32 (0.029 and 0.057 without a pull, 0 with it). The spiral's waves reached into the digits up to 1/16 (0.21, 0.17, and 0.08) and stopped short of them from 1/8, so `RD_MASK_PULL` is 4,096 (0.125).

**Robustness.** The three presets on the 100 × 114 and 120 × 136 grids with seeds 42 and 1234 reached fills of 0.92 to 0.99 at 1,500 steps, and the spiral changed by 0.018 to 0.019 per step at 10,000 steps. The C core reproduces the prototype: `scripts/fill.py grids --presets fhn-stripes` gives a block fill of 0.97 at 1,500 steps on the watch grid, and [Float32 precision comparison](float-precision.md) measures both implementations cell by cell.
