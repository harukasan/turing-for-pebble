# Shared C core

## Grid and memory contract

| Grid      | Storage per cell                         |   Fields | Row buffers |     Mask | Total caller allocation |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------: | ----------: | -------: | ----------------------: |
| 120 × 136 | one unsigned 16-bit Q15 word per species | 65,280 B |     4,320 B | 16,320 B |                88,175 B |

The table holds for both models. FitzHugh–Nagumo stores its two species in the same Q15 words. The grid (`RD_GRID_WIDTH` × `RD_GRID_HEIGHT` in `core/rd.h`) is not an integer fraction of the display, so it is shown with interpolated rendering (below). The total includes a 96-byte control structure, a 2,156-byte rendering area, and up to 3 bytes of alignment padding. The rendering area holds an 800-byte RGBA output row, a 516-byte display lookup table, 600 bytes of column tables for interpolation (the left column, the weight, and the right column of every display column), and a scratch row of 2 bytes per grid column. Row buffers hold one 32-bit error diffusion residual row per species and a step scratch of seven 32-bit words per grid column: a row of 20-fold Laplacian sum pairs, a row of new value pairs, and the old rows above and at the row and the original first row as 16-bit codes. The rows below are read in place. Consumers must query `rd_bytes` and `rd_memory` instead of relying on these recorded values. The core performs no allocation. The caller owns a single contiguous block and frees the original pointer, not the potentially aligned state pointer returned by `rd_init`.

Only the row being rewritten is copied. The saved first row supplies the periodic boundary when the last row is updated.

## Numerical definition, version 5

Version 5 adds a second model, FitzHugh–Nagumo. The Gray–Scott definition is that of version 4, unchanged: every Gray–Scott field hash of version 4 is still in `tests/golden-hashes.txt`. Each field has one model, chosen at initialization (`rd_init_model`), with a parameter vector of Q15 integers (`RD_PARAM_MAX` = 10 entries, of which Gray–Scott uses 5 and FitzHugh–Nagumo 9).

### Gray–Scott

The Gray–Scott model uses an explicit Euler step and periodic boundaries:

```text
A' = clamp(A + dt × (Da × lap(A) − A × B² + feed × (1 − A)))
B' = clamp(B + dt × (Db × lap(B) + A × B² − (feed + kill) × B))
```

Its parameter vector is feed, kill, da, db, dt, each Q15 in [0, 32768], where `RD_Q15_ONE` = 32,768 means 1. Storage rounding uses the error diffusion described below. The arithmetic of a step depends on the storage.

A Gray–Scott step computes on the Q15 codes with 32-bit arithmetic (`react_codes`). The Laplacian stays the exact 20-fold integer sum `S = 4 × axial_sum + diagonal_sum − 20 × center` of the codes (`laplacian_sums`). Each diffusion coefficient is folded with the 1/20 of the Laplacian into `fold(D) = (D + 10) / 20` in integer division, so the core computes with the effective coefficient `fold(D) × 20 / 32768`, within 10/32768 of the requested one (0.99976 of the request for Da = 1 and Db = 0.5). The reaction `A × B²` is exact to the Q30 unit: `A × B` splits at bit 15 into `hi × 2¹⁵ + lo`, and the reaction is `hi × B + floor(lo × B / 2¹⁵)`. The rates, in Q30, are

```text
rate_A = fold(Da) × S_A − A B² + feed × (1 − A)
rate_B = fold(Db) × S_B + A B² − (feed + kill) × B
```

`rate_A` always fits a signed 32-bit integer, because `fold(Da) × S_A` stays below 2³⁰ in magnitude. Of `rate_B` only the final subtraction can leave the range, when `feed + kill` exceeds 1, and the result then saturates at −2³¹, which the clamp below turns into B = 0 in every such case, as the exact value would. With `dt` = 1 the new value in Q24 is the code times 2⁹ plus the rate rounded half up to Q24, `(rate >> 6) + ((rate >> 5) & 1)`. With another `dt` it is the code times 2⁹ plus `(rate × dt + 2²⁰) >> 21` in 64 bits, the same rounding. The result then passes through error diffusion, which clamps it to the codable range of its storage.

### FitzHugh–Nagumo

FitzHugh–Nagumo runs with the same explicit Euler step, periodic boundaries, and nine-point Laplacian:

```text
u' = u + dt × (Du × lap(u) + ru × (u − u³ − v + k) − pull × (u − rest))
v' = v + dt × (Dv × lap(v) + rv × (u − av × v))
```

u is the displayed and masked species, v the inhibitor. `pull` is 0 away from a mask (below). The parameter vector is du, dv, ru, rv, av, k, dt, rest, init (`RD_FHN_PARAMS` = 9). k and rest are Q15 in [−32768, 32768], ru is Q15 in [0, 8192] (`RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, 0.25, which the int32 bound below needs), init is the integer 0 or 1, and the others are Q15 in [0, 32768]. rest is the resting value of u: the initial uniform field, the value masked cells hold, and the target of the pull. It must be the resting point of the other parameters, the root of u − u³ − u / av + k = 0, which is unique for 0 < av ≤ 1 because the left side decreases in u (0 for av = 0). Any other value makes the masked cells a source that draws a band along the digits or, in the excitable regime, sends waves from them, and lets the initial field drift as a whole. The core takes rest as given, and the Web demo, the settings page, and the presets derive it with `restingPoint` of `lib/presets.ts`, by bisection over [−2, 2], so no slider or setting sets it on its own. The preset values in Q15 are −9584 for `fhn-hex` and −21936 for `fhn-spiral`, whose Q13 values are those of the earlier −9585 and −21935, so the fields are unchanged. init selects the initial condition.

**Storage.** Both species x ∈ [−2, 2] are stored as the Q15 code of the fraction s = (x + 2) / 4, so x = 4 × code / 32768 − 2 and the code minus 16,384 is x in Q13 (`x13`). Plane 1 holds u and plane 0 holds v, so `rd_get` species 1 is u and species 0 is v, each returned as the fraction s in Q24. The codes clamp x to [−2, 2]. Error diffusion and its nearest-code rounding are those of the Gray–Scott Q15 modes.

**Arithmetic** (`react_fhn_codes` in `core/rd.c`), in 32 bits on the codes, with `>>` an arithmetic shift (a floor):

```text
u13 = code_u − 16384            v13 = code_v − 16384
uu  = (u13 × u13) >> 13         uuu = (uu × u13) >> 13
k13 = (k + 2) >> 2              rest13 = (rest + 2) >> 2
rate_u = fold(Du) × S_u + ru × (u13 − uuu − v13 + k13) − pull × (u13 − rest13)
rate_v = fold(Dv) × S_v + rv × (u13 − ((av × v13) >> 15))
```

`S` and `fold(D) = (D + 10) / 20` are the 20-fold Laplacian sum of the codes and the folded diffusion coefficient of Gray–Scott. k and rest are rounded to Q13 every step, so the effective values are within 2/32768 of the request, in steps of 4/32768. A Q15 coefficient times a Q13 value of x is directly the Q30 rate of the stored fraction: s changes by a quarter of x, and Q15 to Q13 is the same factor of 4. The new value is formed as for Gray–Scott: the code times 2⁹ plus the rate rounded half up to Q24 with dt = 1, or `(rate × dt + 2²⁰) >> 21` in 64 bits otherwise.

**Both rates fit a signed 32-bit integer without saturation.** `|fold(D) × S| ≤ 1638 × 655,360 = 1,073,479,680 < 2³⁰`. For |x| ≤ 2, |x − x³| ≤ 6, so `|u13 − uuu| ≤ 49,152`: the maximum is reached at |x| = 2, where the floors are exact, and one Q13 step inward lowers |x − x³| by about 11 units while the floors move it by at most 3. The bracket of `ru` is then at most 49,152 + 16,384 + 8,192 = 73,728 in magnitude, which `ru ≤ 8192` turns into at most 603,979,776. With a pull of at most 8,192 (the mask uses at most 4,096), `|pull × (u13 − rest13)| ≤ 8,192 × 24,576 < 2²⁸`. The sum is at most 1,878,786,048 < 2³¹. For v, `|u13 − ((av × v13) >> 15)| ≤ 32,768`, so `|rate_v| ≤ 1,073,479,680 + 32,768 × 32,768 = 2,147,221,504 < 2³¹`. The oracle in `tests/core.c` computes both rates in 64 bits from this definition and asserts the bound, including the extreme cells of either sign.

**Initialization.** `rd_init_model` fills u = rest (code `16384 + rest13`) and v = rest / av as `v13 = rest13 × 32768 / av` in C integer division (truncation toward zero), or v = k (`k13`) if av is 0, where the v equation forces u = 0 and the u equation then balances at v = k, clamped to [−16384, 16384]. With init 0 the 24 LCG disks of Gray–Scott are then seeded, each writing u = rest + 0.5 (the resting code plus 4,096) and leaving v, which is also what `rd_seed` writes. With init 1 no LCG is used: a broken wave is written in display coordinates, u = 1 (code 24,576) in display rows 190–197 and v = 1 in rows 174–189, both in columns 0–99, each grid cell sampling the display coordinate it covers. The refractory v band lets the wave run only downward, and its free ends curl into a pair of counter-rotating spirals below the date line. A torus cannot hold a single spiral, so a pair is the smallest defect set. Q15 codes carry no dither, so a uniform field stays exactly uniform, and one whose rest is not an exact fixed point relaxes uniformly.

**Masked cells** hold u at the resting code `16384 + rest13`, which `rd_mask` writes at once and every step writes without encoding, with the share rules of Gray–Scott B. v is computed and encoded as usual. At mask levels 1 to 5, u is pulled toward rest with `pull = RD_MASK_PULL × (5 − level) / 5` in C integer division, `RD_MASK_PULL` = 4,096 (0.125), so level 5 has no pull and rows away from the mask skip it. Holding u at the dark plateau −1 instead would pull v of the neighboring cells below its rest and let a bright band rise along the digits, and in the excitable regime any value other than rest would turn the digits into a wave source. Fixing u at rest avoids both by construction. These alternatives were reasoned out, not run. The strength of the pull was measured ([Parameter search](#parameter-search)).

**Rendering** maps the Q24 value of the displayed species through `intensity = clamp(round_shift((value − offset) × gain, 9), 0, 32768)`, with offset 0 and gain 3 for Gray–Scott (3 B, bit for bit the rendering of version 4) and offset 2²³ and gain 4 for FitzHugh–Nagumo, so the intensity is u: u ∈ [0, 1] spans the palette, and u ≤ 0, the resting value of the excitable regime and the dark phase of the Turing patterns, shows the background color.

### History

Version 4 moved the step from 64-bit Q24 arithmetic to the 32-bit arithmetic above, which the precision harness measured at the same Float32 error and the watch at 21% less time per step (27% with the later three-pass loop). [Storage precision study](precision-optimization.md) records the comparison. Version 5 added FitzHugh–Nagumo and the parameter vector, and left every Gray–Scott result of version 4 unchanged.

The Q15 code of a Q24 value v decodes as `code × 512`. The nearest code is `(v + 256) >> 9`, halfway upward.

### Error diffusion

Every write uses Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion (`encode_cell` and `finish_row` in `core/rd.c`). The value to be written, plus the shares it received from already written neighbors, is clamped to [0, 1] and rounded to the nearest code, taking the next code when its fraction of the step is at least 1/2. The residual, the clamped value minus the decoded code, is divided with C integer division: 7/16 to the right neighbor, 3/16 to the lower-left neighbor, 5/16 to the neighbor below, and the remainder to the lower-right neighbor. Cells are written row by row from the top and column by column from the left. The lower-left share of column 0 wraps to the last column and the lower-right share of the last column wraps to column 0 of the next row. The right share of the last column carries into column 0 of the next row. Shares held between cells are reset at the start of each step. The residual rows persist: shares given below the last row are applied to row 0 of the next step. A uniform field has zero residuals and remains exact.

### Masked cells

`rd_mask` takes a cell bitmap and derives a mask level for every cell: 0 in a masked cell, else the chessboard distance in cells to the nearest masked cell, capped at `RD_MASK_RAMP` (5). Distances do not wrap around the field edges. Only the band of rows within 4 rows of a masked cell is derived and read. The levels take one byte per cell (the mask column above). A ring of horizontal distance rows in the step scratch, which is free between steps, derives them in `rd_mask`. Deriving them during every step instead saved memory but cost 13% of the step time on the watch, and a 3-bit store left too little heap at 134 × 152, so both were dropped.

In Gray–Scott the kill rate of a cell rises linearly from the parameter kill at level 5 to `RD_MASK_KILL` (2,458, 0.075) at level 0: `kill + (2458 − kill) × (5 − level) / 5` with C integer division. The reaction of every cell uses its own kill. A is computed and encoded as usual everywhere. B of a masked cell is held at code 0: `rd_mask` writes it at once, and every step writes 0 without encoding. The B shares it received from the row above and the right share of the cell to its left are dropped, it gives no share of its own, and its B residual entry is the lower-right share of the cell to its left plus the lower-left share of the cell to its right, which pass through to the row below.

With A left free and the kill raised around the mask, B cannot grow near the digits and the pattern fades out a few cells before them. Holding A at 1 as well was tried first: the mask then supplies fresh A to its edge and a bright band forms along it. Only the rows that contain a level below 5 read the levels, so a step without a mask is unchanged. `rd_seed` skips masked cells. The levels are not part of `rd_hash`, but the golden hashes with a clock mask pin this rule. FitzHugh–Nagumo uses the same levels and share rules with u held at its resting code and pulled toward rest instead of a raised kill rate ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)).

Error diffusion makes the write deterministic and order dependent. The full-screen oracle in `tests/core.c` reproduces the same order. It conserves the encoded mass of a row exactly, which `tests/core.c` checks, and moves the quantization error to high spatial frequencies that diffusion removes within a few steps.

## Initialization

The initial Gray–Scott equilibrium is A=1 and B=0. A 32-bit LCG places 24 disks in a common 200 × 228 display coordinate system. Disk radii range from 4 through 9 display pixels. Grid cells sample their corresponding display coordinates, with periodic distance used at edges. Seeded values are A=0.5 and B=0.25, written directly as exact Q15 codes. Grid resolution still changes the physical scale of emergent patterns. FitzHugh–Nagumo starts from u = rest and v = rest / av and seeds the same disks with u = rest + 0.5, or writes its broken wave with init 1 ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)).

A mask has one bit per grid cell in rows of `(width + 7) / 8` bytes, cell x in bit x % 8 of byte x / 8.

`core/clock_mask.c` builds the mask of the clock digits, shared by the watch and the Web. `cm_build(mask, width, height, font, hour, minute, year, month, day, halo, date)` draws HH:MM and, when `date` is nonzero, YYYY.MM.DD with the glyphs of font set `font` (`CM_FONT_LECO` or `CM_FONT_BITHAM`) as the watch places them, each line centered by the sum of its advances. The time line's text box starts at `cm_time_top(font, date)`: the layout's time top with the date, and without it the top that centers the ink of the time glyphs on the display, which `scripts/gen-clock-glyphs.py` computes into the layout table. It accepts grid widths from 50 to 200 with `height = width × 228 / 200`. Each glyph pixel is widened by a square of `halo` display pixels (at most `CM_MAX_HALO`), clipped to the display, and every grid cell holding the center of such a pixel, `floor((2x + 1)·w / 400)`, is marked. That equals the earlier `x·w / 200` rule at widths 100 and 200. It returns −1 without writing for arguments out of range. `cm_draw(row, context, color, font, hour, minute, year, month, day, date)` sets the same glyph pixels in a display through a row callback, which the watch uses to draw its clock text. The rows map by the same rule with the height and 456. `cm_draw` returns −1 for a NULL row callback. `cm_bytes`, `cm_layout` (font keys and text boxes), and `cm_font_available` complete it. The glyph tables in `core/clock_glyphs.h` are generated. A build that defines `RD_FONT` compiles only that set.

The analog face uses angles in units of 1/1440 of a turn (`CM_TURN`), clockwise from 12 o'clock. `cm_build_analog(mask, width, height, font, hour_angle, minute_angle, year, month, day, halo, date)` draws the hour hand (length 54, radius 3), the minute hand (length 80, radius 2), and the center disk (radius 5) around display pixel (100, 104), then, when `date` is nonzero, the date line of the font set centered at `cm_analog_date_top(font)`, and marks cells with the halo and cell rule of `cm_build`. A hand's tip is the center plus `L × sin(a)` across and minus `L × cos(a)` down, each rounded half away from zero from a Q14 sine. The sine table `CM_SIN_Q14` in `core/clock_trig.h` (generated by `scripts/gen-clock-trig.py`) holds `round(sin(2πk / 1440) × 16384)` for k = 0 to 360, 722 bytes, and the other quadrants fold onto it, so mirrored angles give exactly mirrored tips. A hand is the capsule of the segment from the center to the tip: a pixel belongs to it when its squared distance to the segment is at most the squared radius, tested in exact 32-bit integer arithmetic without division. Each row of a capsule is one run, scanned from the pixel nearest the segment. A halo 0 mask of width 200 has one cell per display pixel, so it is exactly the pixel bitmap of the face, which the Web draws, and `cm_draw_analog` sets the same pixels, which `tests/core.c` checks with the geometry of all 720 times, mirror symmetry, and the capsule against the distance test. `cm_sweep_angle` moves `elapsed_ms / duration_ms` of the way with a quadratic ease-out, `1 − (1 − t)²` in steps of 1/1024, along the signed shorter difference, rounding the offset as `(delta × eased + 512) / 1024` with C division. It returns `from` until the sweep starts and exactly `to` from its end. A build without `RD_FACE` compiles both faces, as the watch builds do for the face setting. One that defines `RD_FACE` as 0 compiles no analog code, and one that defines it as 1 compiles only the date line of its font set, so `cm_build` and `cm_draw` return −1 there. The watch compiles the analog code for size. The golden hashes with an analog mask pin the table and the rasterizer.

Rendering flags are `RD_ROW_QUANTIZE` (1, round each channel to the RGB2 levels, implied by `rd_row_rgb2`) and `RD_ROW_BILINEAR` (2). Without `RD_ROW_BILINEAR`, each display pixel shows the grid cell that covers it. With it, the displayed species (B, or u) is interpolated at the pixel center before coloring. Display pixel x lies at grid position u = ((2x + 1)·w − 200) / 400. With q = floor(256u), the left cell is floor(q / 256) modulo w, periodic like the field, and the right cell gets the weight q mod 256. Rows use the same rule with h and 228. The two grid rows are first lerped vertically in Q15 with rounding half up, then each pixel lerps horizontally the same way. The result is colored through a 513-entry table of 64-value Q15 buckets, with the exact color computed for the few buckets that straddle a color step. Because interpolation happens before the RGB2 quantization, the color steps that outline the stripes fall at display-pixel positions between cells, so a grid that is not dot-by-dot shows smooth stripe edges instead of uneven 1- and 2-pixel blocks.

Palettes (`RD_PALETTE_*`) are 0 lime, 1 cyan, 2 monochrome, 3 viridis, 4 magma, 5 plasma, 6 inferno, 7 cividis, 8 turbo, and 9 custom, with `RD_PALETTE_COUNT` after them. The display intensity is B × 3 for Gray–Scott and u for FitzHugh–Nagumo (the Q24 value minus the model's offset, times its gain), rounded to Q15 and clamped to [0, 1.0] ([FitzHugh–Nagumo](#fitzhughnagumo)). Monochrome is white from an intensity of 45% and black below it. Every other palette has 2 to 8 RGB stops (`RD_PALETTE_MAX_STOPS`) equally spaced from intensity 0 to 1.0. With n stops the position is intensity × (n − 1), its multiples of 2^15 select the segment (the last one at 1.0), and each channel is `low + floor(((high − low) × fraction + 2^14) / 2^15)`, the nearest integer to the exact interpolation with halves rounded up. Lime and cyan have two stops and keep the colors of the former endpoint formula, which `tests/core.c` checks against the hashes of the earlier renderer. The built-in stops are defined in `lib/palettes.ts`, and `scripts/gen-palettes.mjs` writes them to the generated `core/palettes.h`, whose staleness `mise run test-core` checks. The colormaps are eight samples of matplotlib's 256-entry tables, with their origins and licenses in [licenses/palettes.txt](../licenses/palettes.txt). `rd_palette(state, rgb, count)` sets the stops of the custom palette from `count` RGB triples and drops a lookup table built for it. `rd_init` gives the custom palette the lime stops. `rd_row_rgb2` fills a lookup table of 64-code buckets, with a fallback for the few buckets that straddle a color step, the first time a palette is requested and reuses it afterwards. The model of a state is fixed from `rd_init_model` on, so the tables never need to be rebuilt for another model. The watch renders its rows straight into the framebuffer with `rd_row_rgb2_into`. `core/rd.h` also defines `RD_VERSION`, `RD_Q15_ONE`, `RD_VALUE_BITS`, `RD_VALUE_ONE`, `RD_DISPLAY_WIDTH`, `RD_DISPLAY_HEIGHT`, `RD_GRID_WIDTH`, `RD_GRID_HEIGHT`, `RD_ROW_BYTES`, `RD_SPECIES_A` / `RD_SPECIES_B`, the model numbers `RD_MODEL_GRAY_SCOTT`, `RD_MODEL_FHN`, and `RD_MODEL_COUNT`, the vector lengths `RD_PARAM_MAX`, `RD_GRAY_SCOTT_PARAMS`, and `RD_FHN_PARAMS`, `RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, and the mask constants `RD_MASK_RAMP`, `RD_MASK_KILL`, and `RD_MASK_PULL` for C callers. A returned rendering row is overwritten by the next row call. The Web adapter copies each row into a persistent ImageData. The watch adapter writes RGB2 values directly into the OS framebuffer and owns no full-screen image.

Validation occurs before mutations. Invalid coefficients (a vector of the wrong length or with an entry out of its range), unknown models, seed coordinates, step counts, output parameters, and undersized initialization blocks leave state unchanged. Callers must supply live owned memory and a valid state pointer. Arbitrary dangling pointers are outside the C API contract. The API is not thread-safe for simultaneous use of one state.

## Implementation notes

These choices change no result. `tests/golden-hashes.txt` records the field hashes of the definition and `scripts/check-optimization.sh` checks every build against them.

- Each row's Laplacians are computed in one pass, sharing the vertical sums `up[x] + down[x]` between neighboring columns, with the wrapped first and last columns handled outside the loop, as 20-fold sums of the codes written to alternate entries of a row of pairs (`laplacian_sums` with a stride of 2).
- The coefficients, the residual row pointers, and the diffusion shares live in a stack context during a step, so the stores into the planes cannot force the compiler to reload them.
- The nearest Q15 code, halfway upward, is computed directly as `(value + 256) >> 9` instead of from the floor code and a comparison.
- `rd_step` works on the codes themselves. The stored rows below the row being written are still old and are read in place, only the old row being rewritten is copied as 16-bit codes, and the 20-fold Laplacian sums are taken on the codes into a row of pairs. Each row is then written in three passes with few live values each, so that the Cortex-M keeps them in registers: `react_row` computes the new values of both species into a row of pairs, and `encode_row` writes one species with its error diffusion. On the watch the three passes took 13.1 ms per step against 14.25 ms for one loop over both species, whose spills cost about a fifth of its instructions. On a desktop CPU the single loop is faster, so the shared core pays about 1.5 times the host step time for the watch gain. Fusing the Laplacian sums into the cell loop, unrolling it twice, compiling for the Cortex-M33, and a branch-free form of the 64-bit rounding each measured slower or equal on the watch. The passes are inlined twice, for rows inside and outside the mask band, so rows away from the digits run without the level lookups.
- The interpolated renderer takes its column tables (left and right columns, weights) and the lookup table once per row, and lerps as `l + floor(((r − l)·w + 128) / 256)`, which equals the two-weight form with one multiply. `rd_row_rgb2_into` writes a row straight into a caller buffer such as a framebuffer row.
- The value that error diffusion gives the cell to the left below is held in a register and stored once, when the below-left share of the next cell is known, instead of a read-modify-write per cell. The oracle in `tests/core.c` keeps the plain form of the rule on its own residual row.
- The grid size is a compile-time constant, so the row loops have fixed trip counts. With nearest sampling, display pixel x shows cell `x × 120 / 200`. The interpolated renderer has a loop specialized for full rows of 120 cells, where display pixels 5k to 5k + 4 have the constant weights 204, 102, 0, 153, and 51. `render_rgb2`, `value_argb8`, and the glyph traversal are kept out of line so that the watch build holds one copy of each, while the row passes are always inlined. A watch build defines `RD_RENDER_FLAGS`, which fixes the rendering flags.
- Error diffusion computes the right, lower-left, and lower shares by integer division by 16 with truncation toward zero. This gives the same shares for every Q15 rounding error in [−256, 255] as the former 512-entry table, while removing its 2,048 bytes of static data.
- Optimizing GCC builds, the watch builds among them, compile the clock mask functions (`distance_row`, `advance_ring`, `ring_levels`, and `rd_mask`) with `optimize("Os")`, because mask updates run at launch and on clock changes, outside the step loop. On the watch this saved 828 B, and one diagnostic run of each build measured 0.16 ms more per `rd_mask` call ([Validation](validation.md#mask-size-optimization)). A call from the step loop into these functions would also run at `-Os`. `-O0` builds leave them unoptimized, so `scripts/check-optimization.sh` compares an unoptimized reference with the size-optimized code, and the host tests run the mask at the watch's optimization level. Clang, and so Emscripten, ignores the GCC attribute with a warning that `-Werror` builds reject. Its `minsize` counterpart saved only 39 B of Wasm code in an estimate with clang 18 instead of `emcc`, so clang builds compile the mask functions at the command-line level.
- The same builds compile the code that runs only at initialization and on new parameters for size as well: `rd_init_model`, `rd_init`, `rd_seed`, `rd_set_params`, `rd_params`, `rd_param_count`, `rd_check_params`, and the FitzHugh–Nagumo resting field and cut wave. The parameter check `params_valid` is kept as one out-of-line copy instead of being inlined into `rd_check_params` and `rd_init_model`. On the watch this saved 736 B with the same field hashes, and the step loop is untouched.
- `react_row` chooses the model once per row and runs one cell loop per model, both inlined for rows inside and outside the mask band. Outside the band the FitzHugh–Nagumo pull is the constant 0, so its product disappears. `encode_row` stores the resting code of the model in masked cells. The per-step coefficients of both models live in the stack context, and `advance_code` forms the new Q24 value of either model from its rate.
- A build that defines `RD_MODEL` compiles only that model: the model tests fold to constants, so the compiler drops the other model's cell loops. Without `RD_MODEL`, as in the default watch build, the model is read from the state.
- `core/rd_bench.c` times the phases of a step (the row copies with the Laplacian sums, the reaction, and the whole step) and a full interpolated frame on the watch (`RD_BUILD_DEFINES=RD_BENCH`) and on the host (`tests/bench.c`). Only the watch numbers decide, because the host ranks the phases differently.

The existing Float32 implementation remains in `lib/simulation.ts` as a reference with its original tests. The Web can explicitly select it through `lib/float-simulation.ts` for same-resolution visual comparisons. Pebble builds use only the C implementation.

## Gray–Scott presets

The Gray–Scott presets cover the kinds of pattern the f–k plane offers on the watch grid, one or two of each, rather than every named pattern. Candidates were run with `tests/fill.c` (the harness of `scripts/fill.py`) on the 120 × 136 grid under the LECO clock mask at 13:57 2046-08-29 with the 24 seed disks of `rd_init`, at Da 1 and 0.7 (the thickest and thinnest stripe widths of the watch settings) with Db = Da / 2 and dt 1, seeds 42 and 1234, to 10,000 steps. The fill is the block fill of `tests/fill.c`. 1,650 steps is about the watch's 30 s startup animation at the pace of about 200 steps in 3.6 s ([Parameter search](#parameter-search)), an estimate, not a measurement.

| Preset         | feed   | kill   | Source                                             | Look at 10,000 steps                      | Lowest fill at 1,650 steps |
| -------------- | ------ | ------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------: |
| `maze`         | 0.029  | 0.057  | Mazes of [pmneila's demo][pmneila]                 | labyrinth                                 |                       0.99 |
| `coral`        | 0.0545 | 0.062  | Coral growth of [Karl Sims's tutorial][sims]       | wide labyrinth                            |                       0.94 |
| `mitosis`      | 0.0367 | 0.0649 | Mitosis of [Karl Sims's tutorial][sims]            | spot array                                |                       0.80 |
| `holes`        | 0.039  | 0.058  | Holes of [pmneila's demo][pmneila]                 | dark holes in a bright field              |                       0.99 |
| `cells`        | 0.062  | 0.0609 | [U-Skate world][uskate] of Munafo's xmorphia       | uneven dark cells in a bright field       |                       0.96 |
| `worms`        | 0.046  | 0.065  | type μ of [Pearson][pearson] ([xmorphia][classes]) | worms growing from their ends among spots |                       0.72 |
| `moving-spots` | 0.022  | 0.059  | type ε of [Pearson][pearson] ([xmorphia][classes]) | spots that keep moving and splitting      |                       0.97 |

The sources are:

- John E. Pearson, "Complex patterns in a simple system", Science 261(5118), 189–192 (1993), [doi:10.1126/science.261.5118.189][pearson]. It names the pattern types α to μ of the f–k plane.
- Robert Munafo, [Xmorphia][xmorphia], which maps the f–k plane. [Its catalog of the Pearson classes][classes] lists example values of each type, (0.046, 0.065) and (0.058, 0.065) for μ and (0.018, 0.055) and (0.022, 0.059) for ε. [The U-Skate World][uskate] describes 0.062 and 0.0609.
- Karl Sims, [Reaction-Diffusion Tutorial][sims], with the mitosis (0.0367, 0.0649) and coral growth (0.0545, 0.062) simulations.
- Pablo Márquez Neila, the presets of [`grayscott/grayscott.js`][pmneila] in his WebGL demos (2012), among them Mazes, Holes, and The U-Skate World (0.062, 0.06093).

[sims]: https://www.karlsims.com/rd.html

The patterns scale with the diffusion rates, so some values of the literature behave differently on this grid:

- **Worms.** `worms` is the first example of Pearson's type μ, stripes that grow from each end among inert spots. It grows slowly, so it has the lowest fill at 1,650 steps. The second μ example (0.058, 0.065) grows longer worms but fills only about 0.3 of the blocks at 1,650 steps. pmneila's Worms (0.078, 0.061) and every candidate from feed 0.07 at kill 0.057 to 0.062 lost every seed disk within 300 steps.
- **Moving spots.** `moving-spots` is the second example of Pearson's type ε, spots that crowd each other out, die, and split. It fills the face and still changes 7% to 14% of the display pixels every 20 steps at 10,000 steps. pmneila's Moving spots (0.014, 0.054) keeps moving but fills only 0.67 to 0.80 of the blocks at 10,000 steps.
- **Chaos.** Chaos (0.026, 0.051), Chaos and holes (0.034, 0.056), Spots and loops (0.018, 0.051), and Waves (0.014, 0.045) either spread into flat saturated areas or keep large empty areas, and were not kept.
- **Duplicates.** Solitons (0.03, 0.062), Pulsating solitons (0.025, 0.06), and the former `spots` (0.035, 0.065) give spot arrays like `mitosis`, and so does Pearson's type η (0.034, 0.063), a mixture of spots and worms whose worms break up into spots on this grid. The Default of pmneila (0.037, 0.06) gives a labyrinth like `maze`, and 0.058 and 0.062 looks like `coral`.
- **Narrow diffusion.** At Da 0.16 and Db 0.08, the rates of some Web demos with finer grids, `mitosis`, `coral`, and `cells` freeze into grid-aligned squares on this grid.

The former `thin-line` preset (0.023, 0.052), added to expose narrow-band differences, was removed. Its numbers stay in `scripts/compare-float.mjs` as a numerical reference case with `spots` ([Float32 precision comparison](float-precision.md)).

## Second model

### Choice of FitzHugh–Nagumo

The second model had to add looks that Gray–Scott does not give, fit the fixed-point core and its [0, 1] storage, and run in the watch's time budget. The candidates compared were:

| Model                          | Why it was not chosen                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gierer–Meinhardt               | A division in the reaction and an unbounded range of the activator         |
| Brusselator, Schnakenberg      | Values well above 1 and a Hopf region next to the Turing region            |
| Swift–Hohenberg, Cahn–Hilliard | Fourth-order derivatives, which need a very small dt for the explicit step |
| McCabe multi-scale Turing      | Several blurs per step at different scales, too slow for the watch         |
| Barkley                        | A simplified FitzHugh–Nagumo that gives spirals only                       |
| Kondo–Miura linear saturation  | The lightest alternative, recorded as a fallback                           |

FitzHugh–Nagumo has two species, a polynomial reaction, and bounded values, which an affine map places in the existing [0, 1] storage. With one reaction term it gives both the Turing regime (stationary stripes and spots of even width) and the excitable regime (rotating spirals). The uniform resting field of the Q15 codes carries no noise, so the Turing patterns grow out of the 24 seed disks, as Gray–Scott does, rather than rising from the whole field at once.

### Parameter search

The presets were chosen on the Float32 prototype (`lib/fhn-simulation.ts`) with `scripts/fhn-explore.mjs`, which runs a parameter set on the 120 × 136 watch grid under the LECO clock mask at 13:57 2046-08-29 with a 1-pixel halo, prints fill, amplitude, feature size, motion, chessboard, halo, and connected-region measures at each checkpoint, and writes the renders. Its feature size is the mean length of the lit runs along the rows. The runs that cross the periodic right edge were dropped until the review of the branch, and the figures of the sweeps below were measured that way, a little short of the corrected ones. The targets were features of 6 to 12 cells, a fill of 0.9 by 1,500 steps, no chessboard, no bright band along the digits, and for the spiral a pattern that still turns after 10,000 steps without the digits becoming a wave source. The Q15 values are those of `lib/presets.ts`.

| Preset        | du   | dv  | ru      | rv       | av  | k     | rest    | init | Look                                                         |
| ------------- | ---- | --- | ------- | -------- | --- | ----- | ------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `fhn-stripes` | 0.05 | 1   | 0.015   | 0.0375   | 0.6 | 0     | 0       | 0    | stationary labyrinth, u up to 0.74, lit runs of 6.4 cells    |
| `fhn-hex`     | 0.04 | 1   | 0.02625 | 0.065625 | 0.6 | −0.22 | −0.2925 | 0    | stationary bright spots, about 95 on the watch grid          |
| `fhn-spiral`  | 0.2  | 0   | 0.25    | 0.0125   | 1   | −0.3  | −0.6694 | 1    | a counter-rotating spiral pair below the date, keeps turning |

All three use dt = 1.

**Stripes.** The plan's starting point (Du 0.1, Dv 1, ru 0.05, rv 0.125, av 0.6) gave a clean labyrinth whose u reached only 0.52 to 0.55, too dim for the palette, which maps u from 0 to 1. With v following u through its fast diffusion, the stripe amplitude A satisfies about A² ≈ 4/3 × (1 − 2√(c Du) + c av Du) for Dv = 1 and c = rv / ru, and the wavelength grows as ru^(−1/2) at fixed Du and c. A sweep of Du {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} × Dv {0.5, 1} × ru {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} × rv/ru {2.5, 4} × av {0.4, 0.6, 0.8} reached u = 0.9 with Du = 0.02 and Dv = 1, but that far from the onset the seed disks froze into rings and dots. Sweeps of Du 0.01 to 0.1, ru 0.01 to 0.03, rv/ru 2 to 3, and av {0.6, 0.8} to 5,000 steps, counting the connected lit and dark regions, picked the brightest setting that still forms a labyrinth: u up to 0.74, 16 lit and 3 dark regions, a fill of 0.98 at 1,500 steps, and no motion by 5,000 steps. rv/ru = 2.5 keeps the uniform state clearly damped (ru − rv av = −0.005 at the chosen rates, −0.0075 after the speed-up below) where rv/ru = 2 was brighter but only just damped.

**Spots.** On the stripes setting, k from −0.3 to 0.3 with rest at the matching fixed point (u − u³ − u/av + k = 0) gave dark holes in a bright field for k > 0 and bright spots on a dark field for k < 0, mostly spots from |k| = 0.2, and ever slower growth with |k| (a fill of 0.88 at 1,500 steps for k = −0.2 and 0.39 for −0.3). Raising ru and rv in proportion speeds the growth up. Sweeps of Du 0.03 to 0.07, ru 0.01 to 0.02, rv/ru 2 to 2.5, av {0.6, 0.8}, and k {−0.15, −0.2, −0.22, −0.25} to 10,000 steps picked Du 0.04, ru 0.0175, k −0.22: a fill of 0.96 at 1,500 steps and about 76 spots, a few of them elongated, stationary by 10,000 steps. rest is the fixed point of these parameters, so the masked cells show the same background as the field and no halo forms.

**Speed-up.** On the watch the first seconds of both stationary presets looked still: for about 200 steps, 3.6 s at the watch's pace, the field stays at the seed disks (a fill of 0.32) before the pattern spreads. Scaling ru and rv by the same factor keeps the Turing condition and rest, which depend only on their ratio, av, k, and the diffusion rates, and makes the reaction faster at a slightly shorter wavelength. Factors 1.5 and 2 were compared with the LECO mask on the watch grid. At 1.5 the stripes reach a fill of 0.63 at 300 steps instead of 0.41 and 0.95 at 800 instead of 0.86 with lit runs of 6.4 instead of 7.6 cells at 1,650 steps, and the spots 0.51 at 300 instead of 0.44 with smaller spots (4.2 instead of 5.0 cells at 1,650 steps, about 95 of them). The first 100 steps are unchanged at either factor. The presets use 1.5: ru 0.015 and rv 0.0375 for the stripes, ru 0.02625 and rv 0.065625 for the spots, both at a fill of 0.98 by 1,500 steps and stationary by 5,000.

**Spiral.** The plan's starting point (Du 1, Dv 0, ru 0.25, rv 0.015, av 1, k −0.5) lost its initial wave within 100 steps. A plane-front test showed fronts failing at k = −0.5 and moving at 0.24 cells per step at k = −0.3 and 0.3 at k = −0.2, whose fixed point −0.585 lies next to the knee at −1/√3. Sweeps of Du {0.5, 1} × rv {0.01, 0.02, 0.03} × k {−0.3, −0.35} lost the wave at rv 0.03, broke it up at 0.02, and at rv 0.01 turned but with waves about a screen apart. Du {0.15, 0.25, 0.35} × rv {0.0075, 0.01, 0.0125} × k {−0.25, −0.3} all kept turning for 10,000 steps. rv 0.0125 gave the narrowest bands among them (lit runs of 7 to 10 cells at k −0.3), and Du 0.2 lies between the tighter spirals of Du 0.15 and the wider ones of 0.25. The initial wave was first placed at display row 150, which put one core under the date, so it moved to row 190. ru stays at 0.25, within `RD_FHN_RU_MAX`, so the saturation the plan kept in reserve was not needed. rest is the stable root of u³ = k.

**Mask pull.** `RD_MASK_PULL` values of 0, 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 were compared by the fraction of cells within 1 or 2 cells of the digits with u > 0.5 at 5,000 steps. Stripes and spots needed only 1/32 (0.029 and 0.057 without a pull, 0 with it). The spiral's waves reached into the digits up to 1/16 (0.21, 0.17, and 0.08) and stopped short of them from 1/8, so `RD_MASK_PULL` is 4,096 (0.125).

**Robustness.** The three presets on the 100 × 114 and 120 × 136 grids of the Float32 prototype with seeds 42 and 1234 reached fills of 0.92 to 0.99 at 1,500 steps, and the spiral changed by 0.018 to 0.019 per step at 10,000 steps. The C core reproduces the prototype: `scripts/fill.py --presets fhn-stripes` gives a block fill of 0.97 at 1,500 steps on the watch grid, and [Float32 precision comparison](float-precision.md) measures both implementations cell by cell.
